How This Site Was Built

Build your own website with Claude

You are about to make a real website that anyone can visit. It will have two pages: a first page about you, and a Showcase page where a project you choose will go later. You do not need to know how to code, and it is free to put online. These are the same steps used to make this site. Go in order and take your time.

One big thing to remember: save your chat with Claude. Your chat is part of your assignment.

Before you start

You will need:

  • A laptop or Chromebook. A phone makes these steps much harder.
  • A way to use Claude. Your teacher will tell you which one to use.
  • An email address you can open right now, for your GitHub account. Check with your teacher about which email to use.

Check the age rules with your teacher first. Claude and GitHub each have a minimum age. Use only the accounts your teacher tells you to use. Do not make an account on your own, and never enter a fake birthday.

Stay safe: your site is public

Anyone in the world can see your site, including people you do not know. Things you post can be saved by others, even after you delete them. So leave these off your site, and do not type them into Claude:

  • Your last name
  • Your home address, phone number, or email
  • Your school, your team, or where you are at certain times
  • Your birthday or exact age
  • Passwords
  • Links to other websites
  • Photos of yourself, or anything about your friends or family without their permission

Use your first name or a nickname. Share your interests, your ideas, and the things you make. If you are not sure about something, leave it out and ask your teacher.

1

Ask Claude for your first page

Goal: Claude makes the first page of your website.

A prompt is the message you send to Claude. The better you describe what you want, the better your page will be.

  1. Open Claude and start a new chat.
  2. Press Copy on the box below, then paste it into the chat.
  3. Delete the part in [brackets]. Type your own ideas there, in your own words.
  4. Send it.
  5. Wait for a response. When generating code for a website, Claude may take a little longer. Do not be surprised if it takes a minute or more.
Keep these lines exactly. Add your own ideas.Build my first page as a single file named index.html. Put a nav bar at the top that links to index.html and showcase.html. Make it work on a phone. Only use my first name. Do not make up facts about me. [Then describe yourself and the look you want in your own words.]

Why those lines matter: they tell Claude the file names and links your website needs to work later. A nav bar is the row of links that lets people move between pages. The prompt also mentions HTML files. HTML stands for Hypertext Markup Language. An HTML file contains code that defines the structure of a webpage. The rest is up to you.

Not sure what to write? Answer these questions:

  • Who will visit this page?
  • What do you like, make, or care about?
  • How should it feel? Calm, loud, funny, cozy?
  • What colors do you want?
Example only. Use your own ideas.My name is Sam. This page is for my classmates and teacher. I love drawing comics, skateboarding, and my dog. I want it to feel bright and playful, with orange and teal, and a section that shows my three favorite hobbies.
You'll know it worked whenClaude shows you a page, usually in a panel next to the chat. Not everything will work yet. For example, the Showcase link in your nav bar has nowhere to go because you have not created that page. You will make it in Step 3.
If you see this message, select NoYou may run into the prompt "Claude wants to open a browser window to check the open tabs in Chrome on your computer." Select No.
2

Make it better

Goal: change your page until it looks and sounds the way you want.

Your first try is only a start. Read the whole page, then ask for changes in the chat.

  1. Pick one thing you want to change.
  2. Tell Claude exactly what to change.
  3. Look at the new version.
  4. Repeat until you like it.

Ask for one change at a time. That way you can see what each request did. Here are some you could try:

make the heading bigger add an interests section use these five colors make my bio shorter
Tip: be specific"Make it better" is hard for Claude to act on. "Make the title twice as big and change the background to light blue" tells Claude exactly what you want.

You will build your real project on Day 2. For now, you only need a placeholder. A placeholder is something that saves a spot until the real thing is ready.

  1. Stay in the same chat, so Claude remembers your first page.
  2. Press Copy on the box below, paste it in, and send it.
Keep these lines exactlyNow build a placeholder second page named showcase.html. Give it the same nav bar linking index.html and showcase.html, and the same look as my first page. Add a heading and a short line saying the project is coming soon. I will replace this with my real project later. Make it work on a phone.
You'll know it worked whenThe new page matches your first page, has the same nav bar, and says your project is coming soon.
4

Check Claude's work

Goal: find and fix anything that is wrong. This is the most important step.

Claude can make mistakes. It may even make up details that sound true. You know yourself better than Claude does, so you are the one in charge here. Finding what Claude got wrong is the main skill in this project.

Read both pages slowly, and look for:

  • Made-up facts. Did Claude add a hobby, an award, or a story that is not true?
  • Leftover filler. Look for words like "[Your Name]" or "Lorem ipsum." That is fake text waiting for real text.
  • Private details. Is anything from the Stay safe list on the page?
  • Words that do not sound like you. Would you really say it that way?
  • Typos. Look for spelling and grammar mistakes.

When you find a problem, tell Claude what is wrong and how to fix it. For example:

ExampleYou wrote that I play soccer, but I don't. Please remove that and say that I like skateboarding.

Your chat will show the mistakes you caught, and that is a good thing. You will test your links in Step 6, after your site is online.

5

Save your files

Goal: get both pages out of Claude and onto your computer.

Each page of your website is one file, like a document. To put your site online, you need a copy of each file.

  1. Find your first page in the chat.
  2. Press the download button on the file. If you are looking at the page in the preview, open the small arrow next to Copy in the top corner and choose download.
  3. Do the same thing for your Showcase page.
  4. Open your Downloads folder and check the names.

The names must be exactly:

  • index.html
  • showcase.html

Use all lowercase letters with no spaces. Your site will not work with a name like Index.html or index(1).html. If you download a page more than once, your computer may add a number to the new file's name. You may need to delete the old file from your Downloads folder before you can rename the new one. To fix a name, right-click the file (on a Chromebook, tap with two fingers) and choose Rename.

Why is it called index? When someone visits a website, the browser looks for a file named index.html first. It is the front door of your site.

Stuck? I can't find a download button

You can skip downloading. Ask Claude to show the code for the page, press Copy, and keep going. In Step 6, on your new repo page, click the blue text that says "creating a new file." Name the file index.html, paste in the code, and commit it. For showcase.html, use "Add file," then "Create new file," and do the same thing.

6

Put your site online

Goal: get a real web address that anyone can visit.

You will use GitHub, a free website where people store and share projects. Its service called GitHub Pages can turn your files into a live website.

Part A: Make a GitHub account

Check firstGitHub's rules say you must be at least 13. If you are 12 or younger, stop here and talk to your teacher.
  1. Go to github.com and choose to sign up.
  2. Enter your email and make a strong password. Keep it somewhere safe, and do not share it with friends.
  3. Pick a username. Your username will be part of your web address, so do not use your full name, your birthday, or your school. Something like skatecomics22 works well.
  4. GitHub will send a code to your email. Type it in to confirm your account.
  5. Keep your email private: click your picture in the top right, then Settings, then Emails. Check the box for Keep my email addresses private.

Part B: Make your repository

A repository, or repo, is a folder for your project on GitHub.

  1. Click the plus sign (+) in the top right corner and choose New repository.
  2. In the name box, type your username followed by .github.io. If your username is skatecomics22, you type skatecomics22.github.io. Use all lowercase letters, even if your username has capitals.
  3. Set it to Public.
  4. Click Create repository. You can ignore the options for adding a license and a README.

Part C: Upload your files

  1. On your new repo page, click the blue text that says uploading an existing file. It may also look like a plus (+) button.
  2. Drag index.html and showcase.html into the box together.
  3. Wait until you see both file names listed.
  4. Scroll down and click the green Commit changes button.
What does commit mean?To commit means to save your changes on GitHub. Think of it as pressing Save.
You'll know it worked whenYou can see index.html and showcase.html in the list of files on your repo page.

Part D: Turn on GitHub Pages

  1. Click the Settings tab in the row of tabs at the top of your repo. This is a different Settings from the one in Part A. If you are not in full screen, you may have to click More to find it.
  2. In the list on the left, click Pages.
  3. Under Source, choose Deploy from a branch.
  4. Under Branch, open the menu that says None and choose main. Leave the folder as / (root).
  5. Click Save.

Part E: Visit your live site

  1. Wait a minute or two. It can take up to 10 minutes.
  2. In a new tab, go to https://username.github.io, using your username.
  3. If you do not see your page yet, wait a little and refresh.
You'll know it worked whenYou see your page at your own web address. Your website is live!

Part F: Test everything

Now test your site like a visitor would.

  • Click each link in your nav bar. Does each one go to the right page?
  • Try anything on the page that you can click or use.
  • Open your site on a phone, if you can use one. Does it still look right?

If something is wrong, go back to Claude and fix it. Then follow Step 7 to upload the new file.

Stuck? Fixes for common problems
The email code never came.
Check your spam folder and wait a few minutes. Some school email accounts block outside email. Ask your teacher what to do.
GitHub will not open at school.
Your school may block it. Tell your teacher.
GitHub asks me to set up two-factor authentication.
This is an extra lock on your account. It may ask you to use a phone or an app. Ask your teacher or a trusted adult to help you.
I see a 404 or "There isn't a GitHub Pages site here."
Wait 10 minutes and refresh. Then check that your repo name is exactly username.github.io, your repo is Public, index.html is in your repo in all lowercase, and you clicked Save in Part D.
A nav link goes to a 404 page.
Check that showcase.html is in your repo and spelled exactly right. Capital letters count.
I still see my old page.
Wait a few minutes, then refresh. Your browser may be showing a saved copy.
7

Keep improving it

Goal: learn how to update your site any time.

Your site is not locked. You can change it whenever you want. There are two ways.

For a bigger change: upload a new file

  1. Ask Claude for the change, then download the new file.
  2. Check the name. It must exactly match the file it replaces. If it is called index(1).html, rename it to index.html. If you do not, GitHub will keep it as a separate file and your site will keep showing the old page.
  3. Go to your repo. Click Add file, then Upload files.
  4. Drag in the new file. GitHub sees the same name and replaces the old one for you.
  5. Scroll down and click Commit changes.
  6. Wait a minute or two, then refresh your site.

For a small fix: edit on GitHub

Use this for something small, like a typo.

  1. In your repo, click the name of the file you want to change.
  2. Click the pencil icon in the top right.
  3. Find the words you want to change and fix them. Be careful not to change anything inside the < and > signs.
  4. Click Commit changes.

What you are turning in

Turn in these three things:

  • Your live site link. This is your https://username.github.io address.
  • Your HTML files. These are index.html and showcase.html.
  • Your full chat with Claude. Ask your teacher how to turn it in. If you make a share link, do it when you are finished, because the link only includes messages sent before you made it.

Your chat matters most. It shows what you told Claude, what you asked to change, and the mistakes you caught. Your teacher wants to see you thinking and making choices. A perfect first try is not the goal.

Day 2: Build your Showcase project

Today you turn your placeholder page into something that really works, like a game, a tool, or a creative project. You choose what to build, and you make the decisions. Claude helps with the code.

Your goal: finish one small project that works. A small project that works is better than a big project that doesn't.

1

Choose your idea

Goal: pick one project. There are three ways to do it.

Pick oneRoll the generator on the Showcase page, or look through the full list of 30 ideas.
Change oneStart with an idea and make it yours. A plain quiz could become a quiz about your favorite book series.
Invent oneThink of something you wish existed. It could help you with a hobby, make your friends laugh, or teach something you know well.

Not sure what is possible? Ask Claude for ideas, then pick the one you like best:

Try this if you need ideasI like [your interests]. Give me 5 ideas for a small game or tool I could build in one class. It has to work in one web page with no accounts. Tell me how hard each one would be.
2

Check that your idea fits

Goal: make sure you can finish it.

You can build a lot with one web page: games, quizzes, drawing tools, timers, calculators, story games, and more. Your project still has to follow these limits:

  • It all fits in one file, showcase.html.
  • It works with no logins, accounts, or passwords.
  • It costs nothing to run.
  • It does not ask visitors for names, emails, or other personal information.
  • It uses your own ideas and characters, not ones from a game, show, or movie.
  • It follows the Stay Safe rules, and it is kind. No jokes about real people.

If your idea is too big, keep the fun part and make it smaller:

Too big for one classTry this instead
A huge 3D world like MinecraftA flat, 2D map where you collect items
A game people play online togetherTwo players taking turns on one computer
A high score list for the whole classA best score saved on your own device
A chatbot that answers anythingA story game with answers you wrote ahead of time
A copy of Mario or PokémonA game with a character you invented
Good to knowYour site cannot share information between visitors. If your project saves something, like a score, it stays on the device where it was made. Other people will not see it.
3

Plan the main feature

Goal: decide the one thing your project must do.

A feature is one thing your project can do. A score is a feature. A timer is a feature. Before you ask Claude for anything, write down your answers:

  • What is my project?
  • Who is it for?
  • What is the one thing it must do?
ExampleMy project is a spelling quiz for my classmates. They can pick a word list and type their answers. The one thing it must do is show if each word is right or wrong.
4

Build the first version

Goal: get the main feature working first.

  1. Go back to the same chat you used on Day 1, so Claude remembers your pages. If you can't find it, start a new chat and add your index.html file.
  2. Press Copy on the box below and paste it into the chat.
  3. Replace the part in [brackets] with your plan from the last step. If you copied a prompt from the generator, paste it there.
  4. Send it, and wait for a response. A project can take a few minutes.
Keep these lines exactly. Add your plan.Replace my placeholder page with a real project, in a single file named showcase.html. Keep the same nav bar linking index.html and showcase.html, and the same look as my first page. It must work with no logins, no accounts, and no outside services. Make it work on a phone. Build only the main feature first. I will ask for more later. [Paste your plan here.]
You'll know it worked whenYou can use the main feature from start to finish, and your nav bar still shows up.
5

Try to break it

Goal: find the bugs before anyone else does.

A bug is a mistake in the code that makes something work the wrong way. Claude can say a project works when it doesn't, so test it yourself:

  • Use it from start to finish.
  • Do something unexpected. Click fast, leave a box empty, or type a silly answer.
  • Check the facts and numbers. Claude may make up things like quiz answers or prices.
  • Ask a classmate to try it without any help from you. Watch where they get stuck.

When you find a bug, tell Claude three things: what you did, what you expected, and what happened. Saying "it's broken" does not give Claude enough to work with.

ExampleWhen I click Start twice, the timer runs twice as fast. It should ignore the second click. Please fix that.
6

Level up your project

Goal: explore and make it your own.

Once the main feature works, you can experiment. Save a copy of your working file first. Then add one feature at a time and test it each time.

  • Must do: The main feature works, and it matches the rest of your site.
  • Level up: Add one idea of your own, like a score, a new level, sound, or a theme.
  • Stretch: Only if you have time, add more. Try a harder mode, an animation, or a help screen.

Curious how it works, or what to try next? Ask Claude:

explain how the score works in simple words give me 3 ways to make this more fun is this feature realistic for one class?
Tip: the three-try ruleIf the same problem is still there after three tries, stop adding to it. Make the idea smaller, go back to your working copy, or ask your teacher.
7

Publish and test it

Goal: put your finished project on your live site.

  1. Download your new showcase.html. Check that the name is exact.
  2. Upload it to your repo the same way you did in Step 7.
  3. Wait a minute or two, then open your live site.
  4. Click between your two pages and test your project one more time.
You're done whenYour project works on your live site, your nav bar links work, and you can explain what your project does and one thing you changed.

What you are turning in for Day 2

  • Your live site link.
  • Your showcase.html file.
  • Your full chat with Claude, including your plan, the bugs you found, and the ideas you tried.

Your chat shows your thinking. An idea that did not work still counts if you tried it, learned from it, and made a choice about what to do next.

Words to know

Prompt
The message you send to Claude.
File
One saved item on a computer, like a document or a photo.
HTML
The language web pages are written in. Files that end in .html are web pages.
Nav bar
The row of links that takes people to each page of a site. Nav is short for navigation.
Placeholder
Something that saves a spot until the real thing is ready.
GitHub
A website where people store and share projects.
Repository (repo)
A folder for your project on GitHub.
Upload
To send a file from your computer to a website.
Commit
To save your changes on GitHub.
Live
Online, so other people can visit it.
Feature
One thing your project can do, like keeping score.
Bug
A mistake in the code that makes something work the wrong way.
